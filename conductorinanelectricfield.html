Key Concepts

  • No Ionization: Individual atoms remain electrically neutral; no atoms lose or gain whole electrons. Any charge imbalance is a tiny, collective redistribution of electron density.
  • Electron Redistribution: Delocalized conduction electrons respond collectively to an external electric field with an infinitesimal shift in their average distribution.
  • Surface Charges: Extremely small excess and deficit charges appear primarily at the conductor’s surfaces, while the bulk remains locally charge neutral.
  • Field Cancellation: In electrostatic equilibrium, these surface charges rearrange to cancel the electric field inside the conductor.

Note: This simulator uses a simplified picture. In an actual conductor, conduction electrons are already delocalized across the crystal and do not "leave" particular atoms when an electric field is applied. The field causes only a very small, collective shift in electron density, leading to minute surface charge rearrangements. Individual atoms are not ionized, and the interior of the conductor remains electrically neutral.
